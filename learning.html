The Framework

Four Universal Traits

Every exceptional leader Oxford has studied — across sport, military, arts, and commerce — shares these four qualities.

◈
Vision
Seeing what others cannot yet imagine. Articulating it so others choose to follow.
◇
Authenticity
Consistency between values, words, and actions — under pressure as much as in comfort.
◆
Resilience
Absorbing setbacks without losing direction. Performing sustainably, not just brilliantly once.
◉
Impact
Creating change that outlasts your tenure. Results matter — and so does how you achieve them.
Essential Reading

The Canon

Ten texts that form the intellectual backbone of this programme — the books Oxford cites.

01
On Becoming a Leader — Warren Bennis
The foundational text on authentic leadership. Start here.
02
The Innovator's Dilemma — Christensen
Why great firms fail and entrepreneurs exploit their blind spots.
03
Getting to Yes — Fisher, Ury & Patton
Harvard's definitive guide to principled negotiation.
04
Team of Teams — Gen. McChrystal
How adaptability replaces hierarchy in complex environments.
05
Zero to One — Peter Thiel
A contrarian philosophy of venture creation. Dense. Important.
06
The Righteous Mind — Jonathan Haidt
Essential for ethical leadership. Why good people disagree.
07
Thinking, Fast and Slow — Kahneman
The cognitive science every decision-maker must understand.
08
The Hard Thing About Hard Things
Unvarnished entrepreneurial leadership. No platitudes.
09
Never Split the Difference — Voss
FBI negotiation methods applied to business and leadership.
10
Executive Presence — Sylvia Ann Hewlett
Research-backed guide to gravitas, communication, and presence.
